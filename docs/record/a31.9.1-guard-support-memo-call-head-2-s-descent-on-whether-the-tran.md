# a31.9.1 — guard the descent on whether the translator would look inside

approach, done, on i28.

## What this had to establish

An equivalence in one direction and a cost claim in the other. The dependency
set a definition records must still contain every name it can call, or a late
arrival stops repairing its callers and import order starts changing what a
definition means. And the per-import term must go from linear in the number of
libraries already loaded to flat.

## Where the edge actually comes from

The first version guarded `support_memo_call_head/2` in
`engine/support_graph.pl`, which does have a notion of call position and
already refuses to descend into `quote`, `noeval` and `Error`. Re-measuring
said it changed nothing: 7,821 inferences at the second import and 14,762 at
the twelfth, against 7,842 and 14,863 before. That walk feeds the SCC memo.

The edge is built by `record_translated_supports/3` in
`engine/filereader.pl`:

    findall(Support,
            ( mentioned_symbol(Body, Symbol),
              Symbol \== G,
              Support = function_view(Module, Symbol) ),
            Supports0),

and `mentioned_symbol/2` is three clauses that answer every atom anywhere in
the term. It has no notion of call position at all. `record_translated_alias_supports/4`
makes the same `findall` for the type-alias scope.

## The change

`called_symbol/2` beside `mentioned_symbol/2`, asking what a body can CALL.
A term whose head the translator compiles to a literal is data all the way
down, so the walk answers the head and does not descend. Both support
recorders ask the new question; `type_annotation_supports/4` keeps the old
one, because a type annotation really does care about every symbol mentioned.

`literal_head/1` answers in two parts. The three the language fixes as data
whatever else they are, which is the same list and the same reason
`support_memo_call_head/2` carries; `quote` needs naming there because it IS
a translator form, so the general test would descend into it. And a head the
translator has nothing to compile: no function here or anywhere, no builtin,
none of its own forms. The checks are ordered by how often each succeeds,
since one success ends the conjunction and a real call site is the common
case.

## Why it is sound

The head of a literal term is still answered, and it is the guard for its own
subtree. A name reachable only through a literal head cannot be called before
its head is; if that head later becomes a function, the definition is
invalidated through the head's own edge and retranslated, and the walk
descends then. So import order still cannot change what a definition means,
which is what the whole-body walk was protecting.

`filereader_called_symbols:a_head_that_becomes_a_function_shows_them_again` is
that argument as a test: the same term answers `[zzz_late_head]` before and
`[zzz_late_head, zzz_under_it]` after `fun/1` gains the head.

## What it cost

Twelve one-row files imported one after another into `&self`, reading
`statistics(inferences, _)` around each import. The probe loads
`engine/qlf_boot.pl` before `engine/metta.pl`; see
`ai-notes/a-probe-that-consults-metta-pl-loads-a-stale-qlf.md` for why.

| import | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 7,842 | 5,887 |
| 12 | 14,863 | 5,988 |

    before  T(i) = 702 i + 6,438 inferences
    after   T(i) = 11 i + 5,874 inferences

The 11 is the noise floor a row nobody claims already read, so the term is
gone rather than reduced: importing N Prolog-backed libraries into one space
is linear where it was quadratic. At the 61 libraries `lib/` ships that is
about 1.3M inferences.

## The differential

Disabling `literal_head/1` with a leading `fail` and re-running:

| suite | fails |
|---|---|
| `packages.plt` | `importing_a_backed_library_leaves_the_package_head_alone` |
| `filereader.plt` | `a_literal_head_hides_the_names_inside_it`, `a_head_that_becomes_a_function_shows_them_again` |

and the two cases asserting the walk still descends into a form and into a
builtin pass either way, which is what they are for.

## What this did not change

`recompile_function_in_module/2` still rebuilds every clause of a head when
one clause gains a late dependency, though the support graph records the
dependency per `translated_form`, which is per clause. That is a real
inefficiency and it is untouched here; it stopped mattering for `package`
only because `package` no longer gains those dependencies.
